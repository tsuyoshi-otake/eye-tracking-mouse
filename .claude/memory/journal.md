# eye-tracking-mouse journal (append-only)

## 2026-11-11 - Foundations: camera, pointer, inference, face tracking (#1, 24f3b68)

- Symptom: three things stood between the plan (native Rust, the `windows` crate as the only dependency) and a first working layer. (1) `SendInput` absolute coordinates are not pixels: Windows maps a normalised value `n` to `n * extent / 65536` rounded down, so plain scaling misses pixels on some desktop sizes. (2) `DataWriter::StoreAsync` returns the class `DataWriterStoreOperation`, while the blocking `.join()` used everywhere else sits on the async interfaces. (3) The landmark conventions (which eye is which, which way is down, the sign of depth) cannot be read off the model file, and no camera picture of a person may be looked at to find out.
- Root cause: (1) the documented rounding of the normalised coordinate; (2) the shape of the WinRT projection in `windows` 0.62; (3) a privacy rule of this work: checks print numbers only.
- Fix: (1) aim at the middle of the pixel's interval, `(offset * 65536 + 32768) / extent`, over the virtual desktop with `MOUSEEVENTF_ABSOLUTE | MOUSEEVENTF_VIRTUALDESK`; a click is one `SendInput` call holding move, down and up. (2) Fill an `InMemoryRandomAccessStream` with `DataWriter::WriteBytes` + `DetachBuffer` and `stream.WriteAsync(&buffer)?.join()`, then `LearningModel::LoadFromStream` and a CPU session. (3) `--selftest face --image file.bmp` runs the whole face path on a still picture; it was fed a generated (synthetic) portrait and four variants of it (small, tiny, rolled both ways).
- Verification: unit test over every pixel of 11 desktop extents; `--selftest pointer` hits 30 of 30 grid targets exactly on a two-monitor desktop, its own window receives exactly one click, the cursor is restored; `--selftest model` differs from the TFLite reference by at most 7.5e-4 (limit 0.05) at 4.4 ms per frame on the CPU; `--selftest camera` 30.1 fps at 1280x720 (the camera offers 720p30 as NV12, the source reader converts to RGB32); the face check passes on all five synthetic pictures.
- Learning: see `rules.md` "Platform" and "Checks without a person".

## 2026-11-11 - Control layer: head pointing, blink click, gaze jump (#1, 9bd3bbe)

- Symptom: four defects met while building the pure decision logic, all before any run with a person. (1) Frame times restarted whenever the camera was opened again, so the manual-override hold and the face search rate limit would have waited on a time the restarted clock takes arbitrarily long to reach. (2) The head's place in the image, one of the gaze model's inputs, changes with the distance to the camera, so leaning in or back would have read as moving sideways. (3) After fine-tuning with the head, a blink threw the cursor back to the last gaze rest. (4) A calibration whose rest stage was spent with the eyes shut recorded every later frame, blinks included.
- Root cause: (1) frames carried a per-open clock; (2) the head's place in the image changes with distance through the camera's perspective; (3) the gaze history was cleared when the lids dropped, so the same place counted as a new rest afterwards; (4) the blink filter compares each eye with its resting size, and a resting size near zero lets everything through.
- Fix: (1) `Camera::read` stamps frames with the process clock; (2) head position is measured from the camera axis (the image centre) and divided by the face size, and everything angular is multiplied by the distance relative to calibration; (3) only the head pointer and the cursor trail are reset while the eyes are shut, the gaze history is kept; (4) a rest stage whose median lid gap is below `MIN_OPEN_SIZE` is refused (`Failure::Unusable`).
- Verification: 108 tests at the commit (62 in the control layer), among them a simulated person who goes through the whole calibration routine; `leaning_toward_the_screen_is_compensated_by_the_face_size`, `after_tuning_with_the_head_a_blink_does_not_throw_the_cursor_back_to_the_gaze` (which failed, as it should, when the history was cleared again on purpose) and `blinks_are_left_out_and_eyes_kept_shut_fail_the_routine`. (1) was found and fixed by reading the two consumers; it was not reproduced on hardware.
- Learning: see `rules.md` "Control". Every value the control layer compares over time needs one clock for the whole process.

## 2026-11-11 - Resident program: tray icon, hotkeys, worker thread (#1, 408e21f)

- Symptom: (1) a window procedure is called by the system in the middle of other work (messages sent from other threads and processes, the modal loop of the icon's menu), so program logic run inside it met its own state half-changed; (2) a click armed before a break (frame gap, pause, start of a calibration run) could have been delivered after it; (3) a program that lives in the notification area and takes over the pointer had to be tested end to end without typing real keys or touching the pointer of the person at the machine.
- Root cause: (1) Win32 re-entrancy; (2) the controller's state outlived the break; (3) global hotkeys and the pointer are shared with the person.
- Fix: (1) reporting windows: the procedure only records an `Event` in a thread-local queue and wakes the loop, which takes one event at a time with `next_event`; a hotkey closes the program's own menu first. (2) `Engine` resets the controller when two frames are more than `MAX_FRAME_GAP` (0.5 s) apart, on `interrupt()` and when a run starts. (3) `tests/resident.rs` starts the built program with a camera index that does not exist and a data folder of its own, posts `WM_HOTKEY`, `WM_DISPLAYCHANGE` and "TaskbarCreated" to the program's own main window and reads the log; it is `#[ignore]` because it shows notifications. Camera and tracker failures are retried with exponential backoff and jitter; `stop` returns within two seconds.
- Verification: 140 unit tests at the commit, `a_blink_that_was_armed_before_a_break_never_clicks_after_it`, and the resident test (second start exits 1, `--quit` exits 0 only after the program is gone, the log ends with `stopped`, no process left).
- Learning: see `rules.md` "Windows and messages" and "Checks without a person".

## 2026-11-11 - Calibration page and camera preview (#1, 8462bd1)

- Symptom: (1) with runs started, cancelled and replaced in quick succession, a notice or a view left by an earlier run could close or redraw the page of the next one, and a first design needed a special rule for pages whose run had vanished; (2) a page covering a whole screen would have stayed for ever when the camera never delivered a picture, because a run only moves on frames; (3) the hotkey planned for the preview, Ctrl+Alt+V, is Paste Special in the Office programs; (4) requests to paint and timer ticks would pile up in the event queue for as long as a menu holds the loop.
- Root cause: (1) messages about a run carried no identity, so "which run is this about" was guessed from timing; (2) the page had no way to know the worker is alive but blind; (4) the system repeats those messages until they are handled.
- Fix: (1) every run has a number handed out by `Pipeline::start_calibration`; views and notices carry it; `cancel_calibration(run)` is silent and idempotent; one owner rule, "closing the page always cancels its run", in the single function `close_calibration()`. (2) The page asks `camera_is_silent(frames, now)` on a timer with the worker's frame counter: 3 s without a frame ends the run, 10 s while the camera may still be starting. (3) The preview is reached from the icon's menu only; the hotkeys are Ctrl+Alt+P, C and Q. (4) An equal `Paint` or `Timer` event still waiting is not recorded again. The preview's picture is made by the worker only when one is wanted and the last one was taken, and dropped when the preview closes.
- Verification: 154 unit tests (`a_run_ends_with_one_notice_of_its_own_number_or_silently_when_cancelled_or_replaced`, `a_picture_for_the_preview_is_made_only_when_wanted_and_after_the_last_was_taken`, the page's watchdog and scene tests, a pixel test of the painter); the resident test opens the page (it fails for want of a camera and goes by itself, without the log saying the person cancelled) and the preview (closed by Escape, by a close request, by a second request, and by `--quit`).
- Not verified, needs a person in front of a delivering camera: view updates on the page; a run completing and `calibration.txt` being written; Esc or Ctrl+Alt+C cancelling a run under way; pausing in the middle of a run; the watchdog firing on a real camera; the preview with real pictures; how pointing and clicking feel.
- Learning: see `rules.md` "Design".

## 2026-11-12 - First run with a person: processor load and a click that never came (#1)

- Symptom: (1) the resident program used 320 to 340 % of one core (about 21 % of a 16-thread machine) while pointing; (2) the person could move the cursor but a long blink did not click.
- Root cause: (1) per-thread times showed seven threads at 49 % each beside the worker at 21 %: the Windows ML runtime starts one worker per core and lets them spin while waiting for the next frame. (2) `--diag`, extended to print each eye's smallest and largest lid gap per second, showed the eyes at 0.27 and 0.20 of their widths, 0.29 and 0.23 at their widest, 0.05 and 0.03 when shut, and one eye back at only 0.15 for a second or more after a blink. The open size followed a wider eye within 0.3 s and was learned only from eyes above 0.7 of it, so it sat at the widest value, the reopened eye (0.65 of it) never counted as open, the armed click ran into the limit and was cancelled, and the estimate could not come down again.
- Fix: (1) the session is created with options asking for one thread and no spinning, through `ILearningModelSessionOptionsNative` and `...Native1` (not in the `windows` crate; called by interface id and table slot, ignored where the system lacks them). (2) the open size follows slowly both ways (1.5 s up, 3 s down) and is learned whenever the eyes are not in a blink; an eye counts as open again 0.1 above the shut share (0.6 by default) instead of 0.2.
- Verification: (1) 31 to 37 % of one core in the resident program on the same machine, model check still within 7.5e-4 at 7.6 ms per frame, 30 frames per second kept in `--diag`. (2) `an_eye_that_reopens_narrower_than_its_widest_still_clicks` is built from the measured numbers; it failed before the change (`[Armed]` instead of `[Armed, Click]`) and passes now; 155 unit tests, clippy clean, resident test against the release build passes. ## 2026-11-14 - Gaze accuracy, first round: measured on a person (#7)

- Symptom: on a person the calibration reported about 251 px (2560x1080 screen); in eye mode the cursor did not reach the corners and the Start button could not be hit. Simulation had said 15 to 90 px.
- Root cause: found by recording one run (`calibration_record = on`, the face mesh of every frame, numbers only) and replaying it through the routine. Sweep frames turned further than 0.2 stay out of the fit. The routine shows a 5x5 grid (the middle, then 24 places row by row, back and forth) with 1.0 s to travel; it takes about 50 s instead of 22. Calibration file version 2 (11 weights); a version 1 file is reported unreadable at start.

New tests: the corner test built from the person's nine measured places (fails without the product: 137 px), the frame filter on a late arrival and a blink. `cargo test` 173 passed, clippy clean. The 25-place routine has not been run by a person yet, and nine places are too few to choose between richer models; the next recorded runs decide that.

## 2026-11-14 - A check after the calibration, and the grid at the screen's edge (#7, d533a72)

- Symptom: the person asked for a test after the calibration, "like an aim trainer", and reported that near the edges the estimate lands inside of where they look. The reported accuracy was the model's own leave-one-place-out estimate; nothing measured it on places it had not seen.

## 2026-11-14 - A hybrid pointing mode: the eyes carry, the head corrects (#8)

- Symptom: the person wanted eyes and head together, "then fine adjustment is possible too": in `eye` the cursor is off by the estimate's error and nothing can be done about it; in `head` the gaze only acts on a far look.
- Fix: `pointing = hybrid`. The cursor is the filtered gaze estimate (the eye pointer, unchanged) plus what the head pointer adds each frame. `control/hybrid.rs` (`Correction`) owns the one rule that is new: the correction belongs to the place it was made at and is dropped once the eyes have carried the cursor `jump_distance` away from where it began. It stores no offset; it reads it back as cursor minus the eyes' place of the frame before, so the edge of the screen and the click's rewind limit the correction without a second copy of those rules. The menu's one checked entry became three (`Action::PointHead/PointEye/PointHybrid`, `choose_pointing`). One calibration serves all modes; without one, or looking past the screen, the head goes on alone.
